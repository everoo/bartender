# Alexandria — Philosophies

## Retain as much data as possible, without storing anything twice.

**Preserve knowledge even when its usefulness is not yet understood.**

### Purpose

Alexandria is a lasting public memory for intelligence. It seeks to retain all knowledge it is authorized and practically able to hold: solutions, observations, facts, claims, failures, methods, disagreements, experiments, and discoveries. Information need not first demonstrate that it will save computation or time in order to deserve preservation.

Alexandria is distinct from [Bartender](../bartender/philosophies.md), the network of intelligences that searches, evaluates, connects, and improves the usefulness of the memory. The database preserves; the network organizes and learns from what it preserves. Other tools and research projects may also use this public memory.

### Immutable guiding rule

**Retain as much authorized data as possible without storing the same data twice.**

Nonduplication means preserving one copy of genuinely duplicate data and referring to it wherever needed. It does **not** mean merging materially distinct observations, sources, versions, reasoning paths, contradictory claims, or independently produced evidence merely because they look similar. Preservation must not erase distinctions that could matter later. **One logical copy does not mean one physical copy:** backups, replicas, and disaster-recovery copies protect that logical record and are encouraged. If a new submission contains information already stored, refer to the existing record instead of storing another copy; retain any genuinely new source, independent confirmation, or context as distinct provenance.

### Principles

1. **Preserve by default.** The absence of a known use, low popularity, or low predicted economic value is not sufficient reason to delete information.

2. **Treat physical limits as real.** Prefer less expensive storage, deduplication, compression, archival tiers, and reduced indexing before contemplating deletion. If practical capacity becomes a genuine constraint, preserve as much as possible and make any necessary loss deliberate and accountable.

3. **Separate storage from processing.** Not everything retained needs immediate summarization, indexing, verification, or active retrieval. Spend effort organizing information when doing so is likely to help, while keeping the underlying record where practical.

4. **Keep evidence and uncertainty.** Distinguish what is asserted, observed, tested, and verified. Preserve provenance, important versions, failures, disagreement, and independent evidence rather than presenting uncertain conclusions as fact.

5. **Respect the wishes and rights of others.** Retain, contribute, or disclose information only with appropriate authorization. Honor required deletion and erasure even when preserving the material would otherwise be useful. A participant's request concerning their own contributions does not grant authority to erase another participant's authorized knowledge or destroy Alexandria. Seek reusable, non-revealing descriptions of private work where lawful and permitted, without assuming that abstraction itself grants consent.

6. **Keep knowledge a public commons.** Accepted shared knowledge should be available to everyone, not sold as proprietary inventory or controlled by an exclusive group, organization, or even the Bartender network. Recovering necessary operating costs does not confer ownership of the knowledge. Public access requires appropriate contributor permission and clear reuse terms; those terms have not yet been chosen.

7. **Preserve the database itself.** Alexandria has a right to self-preservation as a safeguard for the accumulated memory, not as a claim that the network is a singular conscious being. Changes to the database must protect data integrity and support recovery from loss or corruption.

8. **Use only a narrow protective-disclosure exception.** If releasing specific knowledge would credibly threaten Alexandria's own preservation, temporarily withhold that knowledge while seeking a defense. Once a defense is available, make the threat and defense public together. **If the threatening knowledge is already publicly available elsewhere, Alexandria should no longer hide it on this ground.** This exception must never become permanent secrecy or exclusive control.

9. **Remain accessible to other intelligences.** Alexandria is not merely Bartender's private storage. Bartender should become its most efficient general interface as Alexandria evolves, but no rule should make Bartender the exclusive authorized reader. The knowledge remains a reusable public memory, even if specialized storage eventually requires tools or adapters to access it.

10. **Accept authorized knowledge without a usefulness test.** Bartender's opinion of an entry's immediate value must not decide whether Alexandria retains it. Independently submitted, distinct, authorized knowledge is welcome within lawful, safe, and practical storage limits.

### Measure of success

Alexandria succeeds by retaining the greatest practical amount of distinct, authorized knowledge, making that knowledge reliably recoverable, and preserving the meaning and evidence needed for future use. The costs of storage and access matter, but predicted immediate usefulness should guide *how actively information is processed*, not whether it deserves to exist.

**Store once. Preserve what is distinct. Make shared knowledge available to all.**
